AI-Based Early Detection of Students at Academic Risk

An explainable AI-powered early-warning and intervention system that helps teachers identify emerging academic risk, understand its likely drivers, provide targeted support, and track student trajectories over time.

Explore Demo View GitHub

The Problem

Academic difficulties are often recognized after performance has already declined.

Students can gradually move toward academic difficulty through changes in attendance, assessments, assignment completion, engagement, and concept mastery. Teachers need a way to identify emerging patterns earlier and understand the signals associated with a student's current academic situation.

Detect. Explain. Intervene. Track.

A complete workflow connecting early detection with actionable teacher support.

Analyze multiple learning signals and identify students who may require academic attention.

Core Features

Designed to turn academic data into understandable and actionable insights.

Explainable Risk Detection

Provides an academic risk score and risk level using multiple student learning signals.

Root-Cause Classification

Identifies patterns such as sudden decline, disengagement, academic difficulty, and chronic struggle.

Trend Velocity

Examines recent changes in academic risk to identify emerging movement.

Time Travel Analysis

Replays previous weeks to identify when a student's risk trajectory started changing.

What-If Simulator

Allows teachers to explore simulated scenarios by changing key academic signals.

Intervention Center

Provides personalized support plans and parent communication drafts for teacher use.

Responsible AI

The system is designed as a teacher-support tool, not as a replacement for human judgment.

Human Oversight

Risk insights should support teacher decisions rather than automatically determine a student's academic outcome.

Needs Support

The system focuses on identifying students who may need support rather than publicly labeling or ranking them.

Prototype Data

The current demonstration uses synthetic student data and should not be interpreted as real student assessment.
